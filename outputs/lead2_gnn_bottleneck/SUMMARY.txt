================================================================================================
  LEAD 2: GNN BOTTLENECK INVESTIGATION -- CONSOLIDATED SUMMARY
================================================================================================

Question: GNNs compress a 2-hop node's info through a fixed-size embedding at
the 1-hop intermediate node (h_v^(1)) before it can reach the target edge's
prediction. The walk-transformer has no such forced compression (every token
stays attention-visible). Does this bottleneck explain part of the walk
model's AUC advantage over GNN baselines?

Scope note: every number below is reported per-dataset, never averaged
across datasets -- the cross-dataset disagreements are themselves findings,
not noise to be smoothed over (see "Tensions" section).

STEP 2: Walk-transformer relay-token MI (all 6 datasets) -- CORRECTED (bug fix)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BUG (user-caught, fixed): original measurement trusted walk-token-position
offset i+/-3 as a stand-in for true 2-graph-hop distance, but random walks
can backtrack (walk_sampler.py only follows directed out-edges; several
datasets have genuine bidirectional edge pairs), landing back on the masked
edge's own other endpoint or a direct neighbor instead of a fresh 2-hop
frontier node. Fixed by verifying true distance via real graph adjacency
(baselines/splits/<ds>.pt) and filtering backtracked/stale pairs.

Final-layer NMI, before vs. after the fix, with % of candidate pairs
filtered as backtracks:

  dataset          NMI (buggy)  NMI (corrected)  % filtered (backtrack)
  bitcoin-alpha    0.32         0.3343           18.9%
  bitcoin-otc      0.24         0.2356           15.3%
  slashdot090221   0.28         0.1717           51.4%
  epinions         0.10         0.0505           31.2%
  wiki-elec        0.08         0.0797           2.9%
  wiki-rfa         0.02-0.05    0.0179            1.5%

Verdict (revised): the fix changes the cross-dataset pattern materially.
slashdot090221 drops out of the "high-NMI" cluster (was 0.28 alongside
alpha/otc, now 0.1717, ~40% relative drop) -- over half its candidate pairs
were backtracks, meaning the original number largely measured "does R
recall the very edge just masked" rather than genuine 2-hop retention.
epinions roughly halves (0.10->0.0505). wiki-elec/wiki-rfa barely move (low
backtrack rates, 1.5-2.9%) and remain the lowest-NMI datasets. Post-fix,
only bitcoin-alpha/bitcoin-otc form a clean "high-NMI" pair; slashdot090221
no longer co-clusters with them despite still having one of the largest
walk-model AUC margins over baselines -- the original "high relay-MI tracks
large AUC margin" narrative is weaker than first reported. This measures
whether the relay token's representation RETAINS 2-hop info, not whether the
final prediction USES it via attention (attention_analysis.py's effective-
distance metric measures that separately, but is itself flagged with the
same unfixed walk-distance bug -- not corrected, marked only per user
instruction -- so read it as provisional, don't conflate with Step 2).

TENSIONS (the disagreements are findings, not noise)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Step 2 (walk relay MI) vs. Step 3 (GINEConv oracle gain) point in OPPOSITE
   directions across datasets: epinions/wiki-elec/wiki-rfa are where the walk
   transformer's relay MI is LOWEST, yet epinions is where GINEConv's oracle
   gains the MOST (+0.0321) and wiki-elec/wiki-rfa show real but smaller
   GINEConv oracle gains (+0.0088/+0.0070) while CSG shows its LARGEST
   negative effect on wiki-rfa (-0.0516). No clean single story ties relay-MI
   strength to oracle-gain strength. [Confirmed still holds after Step 2's
   walk-distance bug fix: epinions/wiki-elec/wiki-rfa remain the bottom three
   by corrected relay-MI (0.0505/0.0797/0.0179) even though their relative
   order shifted slightly -- the tension is not an artifact of the bug.]
2. CSG and GINEConv disagree on whether 2-hop oracle access helps AT ALL --
   GINEConv: positive on 6/6. CSG: positive on 1/6 (slashdot090221 only).
   Mean-aggregation SignedConv layers may already discard 2-hop-relevant
   information in a way that an oracle shortcut can't recover (the shortcut
   bypasses the COMPRESSION, but if the compressed representation it's
   concatenated with also carries decision-relevant noise from CSG's
   curriculum-learning schedule, oracle access alone may not net-help).

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 4/5: Synthetic "inverted fog" graph -- ABANDONED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Goal: a synthetic graph with MI(sign,d=1)~=0, MI(sign,d=2) substantial --
opposite of every real dataset -- to test whether baseline GNN specifically
fails when 2-hop signal is the ONLY signal, while oracle-GNN/walk-transformer
don't (real datasets' d>=2 MI is already too close to zero for any model to
show a meaningful gain from better 2-hop access there).

Construction (generate_synthetic_fog_graph.py, rep1-based): bitcoin-alpha's
real topology, i.i.d. binary latent b(v) per node, sign(u,v) depends on
match(b(u), b(rep1(v))) where rep1(v) is one fixed neighbor of v -- placing
the dependency specifically at d=2 from u (through v's own neighbor) while
keeping it independent of v's own out-edges (d=1).

Calibration (targeted exact discrete MI, replacing edge_sign_mi_vs_distance_
v3.py's pooled-frontier MI which measures a different quantity -- node-
personality homophily, diluted to ~0 by averaging over dozens of irrelevant
frontier nodes -- not what this construction encodes):
  MI(sign, majority-sign of v's own out-edges)        = 0.000493 bits (~0, good)
  I(sign; b(rep1(v)) | b(u))  [conditional, not marginal] = 0.635 bits (substantial, good)
The marginal MI(sign, b(rep1(v))) alone is ~0 by construction (an equality
test between two independent uniform bits carries no marginal information
about either bit alone) -- this is not a flaw, it's why the conditional form
is the correct calibration target. Calibration confirmed correct.

Trained GINEConv baseline + oracle on the calibrated graph:
  GINEConv baseline AUC = 0.5825, GINEConv oracle AUC = 0.5740 (oracle WORSE).

Diagnosis -- ABANDONED, not just a tuning failure: GNN baselines (GINEConv,
CSG) initialize node features x as FIXED random vectors, never updated by
gradient descent (not registered as model parameters) -- b(v)'s identity
never enters the model as a learnable input anywhere. On real datasets this
doesn't matter because the predictive signal is generalizable STRUCTURAL
balance (shared conv weights learn triadic-balance-style patterns that
transfer across nodes). Synthetic-fog's signal instead requires recovering
an arbitrary i.i.d. per-node latent label with no structural regularity --
not learnable through fixed random features and shared weights regardless of
the bottleneck question. The oracle's near-chance, non-positive result
confirms this: a shortcut to a representation that never had access to
informative signal can't help. Separately, the walk-transformer uses a
TRAINABLE nn.Embedding keyed by node id (src/model/model.py:26) and could in
principle memorize per-node latents the GNN baselines structurally cannot --
making any walk-transformer advantage on this graph confounded with
"trainable node embedding vs. fixed feature," not isolable as a pure
bottleneck effect. Decision (user, 2026-06-22): abandon synthetic-fog rather
than invest in a redesign; this report rests on the real-dataset findings
(Steps 0-3) only.

================================================================================================
OVERALL VERDICT
================================================================================================
The 1-hop bottleneck is real and measurable on every real dataset: h_v^(1)
retains genuine but partial (NMI 0.008-0.27) neighbor-sign information
(Step 1), and that retention demonstrably dilutes with degree at the
per-edge level (Step 1b). But whether bypassing the bottleneck (oracle
ablation, Step 3) actually improves AUC is dataset- and architecture-
dependent, not universal: GINEConv gains on all 6 (substantially on
epinions/slashdot090221), CSG gains on only 1/6. The walk-transformer's
relay-token MI (Step 2) is also highly dataset-dependent and does not
cleanly track GINEConv's oracle-gain pattern. Combined with Lead 1's
conclusion (over-averaging is real but modest, ~5-9% norm loss, and not the
primary driver), the evidence here supports a SOFTER claim than "the
bottleneck explains the walk model's advantage": the bottleneck is a real,
measurable information-loss mechanism in GNN baselines, contributes to
GINEConv's gap specifically on a subset of datasets, but does not explain a
uniform AUC margin across datasets or model types. The walk-transformer's
advantage is more likely attributable to multiple, dataset-dependent
factors rather than the bottleneck alone.
